Project Overview

This autonomous driving project demonstrates practical experience with complete simulation-based development and validation of an autonomous-driving software stack. CARLA served as the complete simulation and validation environment, providing realistic vehicle dynamics, RGB camera and radar sensors, configurable traffic scenarios, and precise ground-truth measurements for quantitative validation.

1. System Architecture & CARLA Simulation

The project developed and evaluated a modular perception, prediction, safety-decision, and vehicle-control pipeline for autonomous driving. Key aspects include:

  • Simulation-based Development: CARLA provided the ego vehicle, realistic vehicle dynamics, RGB camera and radar sensors, configurable traffic scenarios, and precise ground-truth obstacle distances
  • Execution Modes Investigation: Both synchronous and asynchronous execution modes were investigated to understand how perception latency and heterogeneous sensor update rates affect autonomous-driving decisions
  • Controllable Scenarios: From dynamic lane-change scenarios with multiple obstacles to isolated single-lane obstacle scenarios for reproducible measurements

2. Multimodal Perception Architecture

The perception system combined three independent sources of environmental information for sensor and model diversity:

  • YOLOv5s: GPU/CPU software perception pipeline with higher accuracy for complex environments
  • YOLOv3-Tiny: Hardware-oriented perception pipeline optimized for edge deployment and resource-constrained scenarios
  • Radar-based 3D Measurement: Independent 3D point cloud generation with configurable field-of-view for ego-lane obstacle detection

Both cameras and radar were specifically configured for autonomous-driving perception: camera resolution, exposure and motion blur were adjusted for visual detection accuracy, while radar fields of view were narrowed to reduce environmental clutter and focus on obstacles in the ego lane.

3. Computer Vision & Geometric Distance Estimation

Beyond object classification, YOLO detections were transformed into metric longitudinal obstacle-distance estimates using projective geometry:

  • Bounding box bottom edge interpreted as road-plane contact point
  • Camera mounting height, resolution, and vertical field-of-view used for geometric projection
  • 2D bounding-box location converted to real-world distance estimates
  • Interpretable perception chain: RGB frame → neural-network detection → bounding-box extraction → geometric projection → obstacle distance

4. Hardware-Accelerated AI & Edge Deployment

A particularly valuable aspect was the heterogeneous software/hardware perception architecture:

  • Model Transformation: YOLOv3-Tiny converted from Darknet to Apache TVM's Relay intermediate representation
  • Optimization Pipeline: 8-bit integer quantization, graph optimization, packing, and compilation for VTA accelerator
  • FPGA Deployment: Deployed on Xilinx ZCU104 FPGA with TVM's hardware simulation mode for validation before physical execution
  • Relevant Skills: Edge-AI deployment, neural-network optimization, quantized inference, accelerator-aware compilation, FPGA/VTA execution, hardware/software co-design

5. Temporal Prediction & Asynchronous Perception Compensation

Addressing a critical real-world autonomous-driving problem—different perception components do not provide synchronized measurements:

  • Projection Mechanism: Used previous distance measurements and temporal derivatives to project expected distance forward to current simulation time
  • Temporal Alignment: Enabled stale or slower measurements (particularly from FPGA pipeline) to align realistically with faster radar and software-perception outputs
  • Consistency Strategy: Historical analysis of approximately five measurements and derivative variability to identify most stable perception sources

6. Fault-Tolerant Sensor Fusion & Safety Mechanisms

A dedicated Safety Mechanism fused the three distance estimates with explicit handling of inaccurate, missing, delayed or inconsistent detections:

  • Conservative Strategy: Fail-safe minimum distance selection for safety-critical applications
  • Nearest-Pair Averaging: Identified two mutually consistent estimates and averaged to suppress single outlier
  • Freshness Strategy: Selected most recently updated valid measurement, protecting against stale outputs and processing delays
  • Consistency Strategy: Evaluated historical behavior and down-ranked sources with noisy or temporally unstable predictions

Documented test case: when YOLOv3-Tiny failed to detect the obstacle while YOLOv5s succeeded, the overall system continued operating because radar and software perception provided redundant valid information.

7. Real-Time Architecture & Concurrency

Implemented a multithreaded Python architecture decoupling perception from vehicle-control logic:

  • Independent software- and hardware-detection threads concurrently performing image preprocessing, inference, and distance estimation
  • Radar callback continuously updating latest spatial estimates
  • Thread-safe shared states, mutexes, and events coordinating data exchange
  • Frame skipping and asynchronous use of latest predictions to avoid blocking the perception/control loop
  • Real-time monitoring dashboard displaying annotated detections, CARLA ground truth, radar/software/hardware distances, and inference delays

8. Closed-Loop Safety Decision & Quantitative Validation

The perception output connected to an interpretable closed-loop braking decision system:

  • Vehicle velocity and fused obstacle distance calculated braking distance under constant deceleration
  • Conversion to projected safety distance with configurable braking threshold
  • Systematic evaluation against CARLA ground truth using MAE, MaxAE, MAPE, residual standard deviation, variance and R²
  • Consistency strategy with asynchronous projected data achieved MAE ≈ 0.137 m with very low error dispersion
  • Demonstrated system-level engineering across perception, embedded AI, prediction, decision-making and autonomous-vehicle validation
